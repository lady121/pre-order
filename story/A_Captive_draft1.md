## Chapter One: The Pig Pen

The wooden fence splintered, a plank crushed beneath a young boy's weight as he fell through, dodging the broom swung at him.

The other four boys had already run, scattering from the fence and leaving him behind. "Get up and run!" they called back.

The boy scrambled to his feet just as the hilt of the broom came down with a *whack* against the plank he'd been sitting on. He slid away, trying to find his balance.

"And stay off my property, or it'll be your heads!" a voice bellowed.

Bellina huffed as the boys scattered down the road.

She went back to the chicken feed. Her long coily hair had fallen around her face with all the movement, and she bent down and poured the feed into the pen, the chickens crowding quickly around her feet.

Behind her, the cottage door opened.

Belle stepped outside, looking toward the road where the last of the boys had disappeared. "Busy morning?"

Bellina kept scattering feed.

Belle walked farther into the yard. "You'll never get married if you're always so aggressive."

Bellina looked up at her, and snorted. "Show me a man worth the trouble and maybe then I'll change."

Belle sighed. "Doesn't it tire you?"

"No. Being soft will only get you taken advantage of. Or worse…"

She was forever beating men away with a broom, and most of the time it worked. When she was around, the village kept its dogs at bay.

All but one.

The sound of leather boots crunching leaves and twigs stopped Bellina mid-rant, her face going sour.

"The boar has come out of its pit."

"Beat it, Gaston." His deep voice grated on her nerves. She dropped the bucket of feed.

She popped her hip to the side and leaned over the fence. "You seem desperate for your daily rejection."

"No one's here to ask for a sow like you," he said, crossing his arms over his chest. Then he held out a bouquet of red roses. "I'm here to seek my lovely rose for her hand. And to save Belle from this pigsty."

Bellina kicked the back of his knee, dropping him into a proposal pose in front of the pig pen.

"Hear that, Daisy? We finally found you a perfect match. He isn't as smart as you, but it's better than nothing."

The fat pig squealed, her face rubbed in mush, the slop slipping between her chompers.

Belle covered her mouth to hide her laugh. Gaston did not look at her. He was looking at Bellina, red to the ears, and Bellina, who noticed nothing but her own victory, wiped her hands on her apron and went inside.

## Chapter Two: Naked as a Mole Rat

[BRIDGE — your fragment "she felt her heart shatter like crystal on stone" has no setup, so I invented one: she decides to change. After Belle's example, she cuts her hair and puts on makeup for the first time and goes to the tavern. Change this if the real reason was different. This is also where Phillip's cut-off "I can tell a masterpiece" line pays off.]

She had cut her hair.

She'd done it that morning with the kitchen shears and a shaking hand, and for a few hours she had felt brave. Then she had borrowed Belle's powders and rouge and her one good dress, and she had walked into the tavern with her chin up like she was walking into a fight.

The room went quiet. Then someone laughed.

Not loudly. That was the worst part. It was the low, easy kind of laughter men use when they don't think you can hear.

She felt her heart shatter, like crystal on stone. Tears filled her eyes, and she turned away quickly, and her face smashed into something hard.

A chest. She looked up into Gaston's face. He looked down at her with a smile, the kind that suited a fox. He wrapped an arm around her shoulder and called out in his big, bellowing voice, "Bellina! What a surprise!"

"Let me go, Gaston." She pushed against his arm. She looked down at herself and regretted every inch of it. She no longer felt brave or pretty. She felt naked and ashamed, like a naked mole rat dashed in makeup. Ridiculous.

"Don't be like that. Stay, have a drink. Rounds are on me!" The room filled with cheers and whoops.

"I said I'm not in the mood!"

She used her whole body weight to shove his arm away and ran out of the tavern, tears falling. Her face was hot enough to burn.

*Was it wrong? Why was it so hard? Why couldn't she, for one second, be pretty?*

"Bellina!"

She turned at the sound of her name and saw Phillip running toward her. She froze. Of everyone, she didn't want *Phillip* to see her like this, so pathetic.

"Don't come any closer."

He hesitated.

"I'm sorry, I need to go." She took off again, but he chased after her and caught her by the wrist. "Wait!"

She turned her tear-stricken face toward him and flushed instantly, looking away.

Phillip stared at her, stunned. "Wow," he said, almost breathless. "You look… beautiful."

"Phillip, you don't have to lie. I know my own face."

"Excuse me for saying so, but I don't think you do. Otherwise you'd know I speak the truth." He released her wrist and pulled out a handkerchief. "Sure, your makeup's a little smudged, but I'm not blind or a fool. I can tell a masterpiece even if it's a little smudged."

She took the hanky and dabbed at her cheeks and eyes, her eyes glossy, her lashes wet.

"It can't hide the beauty of the moon," he said quietly. "Especially when it's surrounded by stars."

Silence settled over them, with only crickets and the beating wings of fireflies.

"I-I'm sorry. That was a bit forward. I didn't mean to make you uncomfortable."

"No. No, that's… very—" She looked at him, the pink blooming in the streetlight, and finished softly: "Lovely."

She played with the ends of her hair, twirling them around her fingers.

## Chapter Four: A Man Like That

[BRIDGE — your scene "He scooped her up by her waist… her back hit against a wall" and "What a show" read like they belong to a **later** Gaston argument. I've kept them in the cottage, in one long scene.]

Gaston had stopped pretending he came for the roses.

He came now with excuses, a message, a question about the fence, and Bellina had stopped asking why he kept finding reasons. She moved around the cottage, wiping her hands on her apron, picking up the tablecloth, and he stood in her doorway as if he owned the frame.

"Hmm. I don't know," she said, light and cutting. "Wouldn't you say a man like that suits a boorish woman like me?"

"A man like who?"

"Phillip." She shook out the cloth. "The villagers would disagree, I think. I'm already 'past my prime.' He's strong and healthy, and he's quite young. Better than marrying some old man." She said that last part pointedly.

"It takes more than youth to make a good husband."

"And how would *you* know what makes a good husband?"

Gaston looked incredulous. "He hasn't even lost his baby fat."

"He's not that much younger than me," she said, nonchalant.

"He's too weak. He wouldn't be able to provide."

"As long as there's money, we can buy what we need. There's no real need for hunting, after all."

"You seem more concerned with who I marry than my father does," she added.

A knock came at the door.

"Oh. That must be him."

Gaston stomped over before she could, but when it opened, there was no one there. Only a package sat on the step, and she waved goodbye to the mailman already heading down the lane.

It was heavy. She hoisted it up, huffing. "I know you have your values for women," she said, struggling with the box, "but I figured that was reserved for my sister."

Her arms felt lighter all at once. Gaston had lifted the box away from her. He held it in one arm, the muscle of him shifting through his shirt, and tossed it onto the table so carelessly it echoed with a hard *thud*. His eyes never left her.

"And women don't make good decisions when choosing a good man," he said, standing over her.

"Then are you a good man?" she said, in an even voice.

He was silent a moment. The distance between them had closed without either of them moving. He didn't answer.

She scoffed. "My womanly instinct says otherwise."

It was something he hadn't taken the time to notice, though it was something no one had. The way her body swayed. The curves that outlined themselves even under her clothes. No, they were never hidden. It was probably what had drawn Phillip to her: he had seen what everyone else ignored. A beauty like a panther, so fierce and dangerous that you focused too much on avoiding it to see how majestic it was.

He took her by the hips and pulled her to him.

Her head snapped up. "Do you mind?"

Pressed against him, he could feel the softness of this callous woman. Her head fell to his chest, and she looked up at him with such fierce, predatory eyes, a lioness suddenly gone meek as a house cat.

*That is dangerous,* he thought.

"A woman shouldn't dress so exposed," he said.
